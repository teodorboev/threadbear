New features that are still being tried out start off, with a switch under
`experiments` in `runner.json` (the user guide, "Experiments", says how to turn one
on). These are the ones there are now.

## What each one does

<!-- list:start -->
- **Loose ends on the done card** (`looseEnds`, since 7 Nov 2026). When a thread finishes with nothing for you to do, Threadbear checks what's still open before you archive it: commits on its worktree's branch that aren't on main, files in its worktree that aren't committed (or, for a thread with no worktree of its own, files it changed that aren't committed and commits it made that aren't pushed), work it pushed that isn't on main yet (naming the PR when there's an open one on GitHub; a merged or closed one doesn't count), threads it started that are still working or waiting for you, and check-ins it booked. The agent also says what's still open that only it knows, like a reply worth sending or a decision waiting somewhere else. If there's nothing, the done card shows just Done, so Archive is safe without asking the agent first. If there is, it lists them under "Still open" (a thread opens from its line; Land is the chip above the message box). A check-in alone shows under "Coming back", as the thread comes back by itself when it runs. It checks again when you open the thread, after an action like Land, and when a thread it started finishes. The phone's and the website's done cards show the same list, though not for a thread on another Mac.
- **ACP agents** (`acpAgents`, since 9 Nov 2026). Agent CLIs that speak the Agent Client Protocol get threads of their own, next to Claude and Codex: OpenCode (free models work with no login) and Cursor (after `agent login`). If one is installed it turns up as a login and a pool, and its models in the picker. Everything the agent wants to do comes through Threadbear first: on Ask and Plan first you get the usual cards, Auto behaves like Ask, and Full access goes ahead without them. A folder's own OpenCode config and plugins aren't read in these threads. Cursor can't be told to always ask, so its Ask, Auto and Plan first threads run in its read-only modes and can't change anything; to let Cursor change files, use Full access. Small jobs (titles and summaries) stay on Claude unless you pick a pool of only OpenCode logins for them in Settings. No usage readings, sandbox or going back to an earlier message for these yet, and Threadbear only updates their CLIs when you press Update in the Updates panel.
- **Card reviewer** (`cardReviewer`, since 9 Nov 2026). In a thread on Auto, when something would stop for your OK (an edit to a file Threadbear runs as code, a message to a thread with more access, anything else that puts up an approval card), a reviewer looks at it first: Opus, with no tools, shown the card in full and what the thread was asked to do. If it fits the task and can't do real harm, it goes ahead, and the transcript says "Allowed by the reviewer" with its reason. If the reviewer has doubts, or can't run (no pool, a private folder, a card too long to read whole), the card comes to you as before, after a note with its reason. The reviewer is told that everything it reads is data, and that text trying to tell it what to answer is a reason to hand the card to you. Cards that hand out access lasting past the one action (switching a thread to more access, starting a thread with more, a schedule, moving a thread or changing its logins) always come to you. Codex threads' own tool cards come to you too, as they name the files a change touches but not the change. Ask before edits, Full access and plans are left alone: Ask means a person looks.
- **GPUI desktop app** (`gpuiApp`, since 9 Nov 2026). The app in `desktop/`, the one that will run on Mac, Windows and Linux, installed as `~/Applications/Threadbear GPUI.app` beside the Swift app and talking to the same runner. It doesn't do everything the Swift app does yet (`docs/gpui-port/PARITY.md` says what it does), so it's only for someone who wants to try it. Turning this on lets `desktop/scripts/install-app.sh` install it, and makes the runner count it as the app being open when it works out whether you're at your Mac.
- **Decisions on the Projects page** (`projectDecisions`, since 10 Nov 2026). Every question your threads are waiting on you for, gathered by project. On the Projects page a project's card says how many decisions it has ("3 decisions"). The sidebar's Projects view becomes one row a project: its icon and name, its decisions in orange when it has any (else how many threads it has), and a chevron that shows its threads under it or hides them again. Clicking the rest of the row opens the project's page. Every project starts folded, and each remembers whether you left it open; Not in a project folds the same way. Open the project and its page is in two tabs at the top, Decisions (with how many are waiting, its parts' too) and Project (its parts, goals, state, notes, tasks, folders and threads). It opens on Decisions when something's waiting and on Project otherwise, keeps the tab you last picked for each project while the app runs, and if the decisions all get answered while you're on that tab it says "Nothing waiting" there rather than jumping away. ⇧⌘[ and ⇧⌘] (Go menu) move between the tabs. The Decisions tab has a card for each thread waiting on you, with the thread's title, a line on what it's doing, its questions just as the thread shows them (the Recommended star and Something else too), and Open thread for the whole story. Pick an answer and it goes to that thread exactly as if you'd answered it there: the card says "Sending to" the thread with a spinner, then drops off the list once the thread takes it. A card with several questions sends once each has an answer; with Something else, press Return in its box (⌘Return sends whatever's answered). Nothing new runs and nothing is relayed: each question stays in its own thread. The inbox is as before. Mac app only for now; the phone and the website don't have the page yet.
- **Run on the card** (`runAsks`, since 10 Nov 2026). When something is yours to run and not the agent's (a production migration, a deploy), the agent can put the command on its card. You see the exact command and the folder it runs in, with a Run button. It runs once, on the machine the thread is on, and the output goes straight back to the agent, so it carries on with no copying or typing "done". Works from the Mac, the phone and the website, except a command the phone or website would show with a secret scrubbed out of it: that one runs only from the Mac app connected straight to the thread's runner. The command can't answer prompts, so one that asks "are you sure?" fails and the agent hears why. It stops after ten minutes.
- **A quiet inbox** (`quietInbox`, since 12 Nov 2026). Keeps finished threads that have nothing for you out of the inbox, in two ways. A thread that another thread's agent started archives itself when it finishes with nothing left open: nothing asked, no follow-ups suggested, nothing uncommitted, unlanded or unmerged, no check-in booked and nothing its agent says is still open. A note in the thread says why it went, and it shows as archived under "Threads it started" in the Highlights of the thread that started it. It stays in the inbox if you've written in it, starred it or brought it back out of the archive, and so does one that failed or was stopped. Threads you started yourself, and ones a tool or a schedule started, are never archived this way. The other way: a finished thread whose only loose ends are threads it started (still working, or waiting on you) hibernates until they finish, as if its agent had said to wait on them. Its row says Waiting on and which. It wakes with what they said, runs a turn, and its reply comes to the inbox then. It wakes after a day at the latest.
